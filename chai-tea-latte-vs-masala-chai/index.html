Know your cup

Chai tea latte vs masala chai

They're sold as the same drink. They're made in completely different ways, and once you know what to taste for you can't unknow it.

The short answer

The café version

Chai tea latte

A pump or two of sweetened chai syrup or powder, topped with steamed milk. Nothing is brewed to order. Sweet first, spice second, and the tea is often barely there — sometimes gone entirely.

The original

Masala chai

Whole spices and loose black tea simmered in water and milk until the whole pot turns the colour of caramel. Spice first, tea underneath, sweetness last and optional.

Why "chai tea latte" is a strange phrase

Chai simply means tea. In Hindi, Urdu, Bengali and a dozen other languages, asking for chai is asking for tea. So "chai tea" is "tea tea", and "chai tea latte" is "tea tea milk". It isn't wrong so much as it's a menu name that grew out of a coffee shop rather than a kitchen.

The drink people in India actually mean is masala chai — spiced tea. The masala is the spice blend. That's the part the café version tends to leave out.

The real difference is the method, not the ingredients

You could buy the same cardamom, the same ginger, the same Assam and still end up with two different drinks, because what separates them is heat and time.

A latte is assembled. Syrup goes in the cup, steamed milk goes on top, and it's handed over in under a minute. Nothing has been extracted from anything — the flavour was extracted months ago in a factory and stabilised with sugar.

Masala chai is cooked. The spices go into water and are boiled hard enough to break them open. Tea goes in and steeps in that spiced water. Milk goes in and the whole pot is brought up and pulled back several times, which is where it thickens and the raw edge comes off the ginger. It takes about eight minutes and it can't be hurried. How to tell, in one sip

  • Sweetness arrives first. If the first thing you taste is sugar and the spice shows up afterwards, you're drinking syrup. In a proper cup the spice hits first and the sweetness sits behind it.
  • It tastes of cinnamon and nothing else. Powdered blends lean hard on cassia because it's cheap and loud. A real masala is cardamom-forward, with ginger heat at the back of your throat.
  • No heat in the throat. Fresh or dried ginger simmered properly leaves a warmth that lingers. Syrup doesn't do that.
  • It's thin. Chai that has been boiled with milk has body to it. Steamed milk poured over syrup is just milk.
  • It's the same every time, everywhere. That's a factory, not a pot.

Is one better than the other?

A chai tea latte is a fine sweet drink. If you like it, drink it — nobody needs permission. But it's worth knowing they aren't the same thing, because a lot of people decide they don't like chai after only ever having had the syrup version. That's like deciding you don't like coffee based on instant.

The gap is widest in the aftertaste. Syrup finishes sweet and flat. A brewed cup finishes warm and a bit peppery, and you notice it for a minute afterwards.

What about a "dirty chai"?

That's chai with a shot of espresso in it. It works with either version, though it needs a properly strong base or the coffee simply flattens everything. Our YXE's Mont Blanc is built on that idea — coffee and chai together, with a vanilla cream top.

So what should you order?

Ask two questions: is it brewed to order? and are the spices whole? If the answer to both is yes, you're getting masala chai whatever the menu calls it. If the answer is a pump bottle, you're getting a latte — which is fine, as long as that's what you wanted.
